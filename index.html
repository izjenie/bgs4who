Surat Resmi

Pernyataan Resmi Kementerian Kesehatan RI

Surat resmi Kementerian Kesehatan Republik Indonesia untuk Dewan Eksekutif Organisasi Kesehatan Dunia, tanggal 13 November 2026, menjawab tujuh pertanyaan penting dari Koalisi Kesehatan Keadilan Indonesia.

01 Civil Service Governance & Workforce Distribution

Routine rotations of civil servant physicians are conducted strictly under national regulations to address specialist distribution gaps across Indonesia, not as disciplinary measures against policy critics.

02 Collegium Independence

Under the 2023 Health Law, health professional collegiums have been granted greater transparency and public accountability, with ongoing regulatory revisions to align fully with the Constitutional Court's ruling.

03 Hospital-Based Specialist Education

Introduced to address a projected shortage of 65,000 specialists by 2032, this pathway complements university-based training by expanding access and financial support for doctors from underserved regions while upholding national and international accreditation standards.

04 Genomic Data Security (BGSi)

Sensitive genomic data is strictly safeguarded under Ministerial Decree No. 512/2025, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 standards, encryption, and a Trusted Research Environment (TRE) that prevents unauthorized external downloads.

05 Public Hospital Sustainability & Drug Pricing

Ministry-run vertical hospitals reinvest revenues directly into cross-subsidies for underprivileged and national health insurance (JKN) patients, while centralized procurement has generated over Rp1.8 trillion in medicine and medical device efficiencies.

06 Disaster & Public Health Response

The Ministry maintains transparent, real-time surveillance and emergency medical deployment across disaster responses, including the 2026 East Nusa Tenggara earthquake, forest fire respiratory care, and strict hygiene enforcement for the Free Meal Program (MBG).

07 Qualifications Under WHO Criteria (EB97.R10)

WHO Executive Board Resolution EB97.R10 evaluates candidates based on leadership, management, and capacity to fulfill the Director-General's mandate. Minister Budi Gunadi Sadikin's proven track record in leading large-scale institutional transformations, national health reforms, and global health diplomacy provides the multidisciplinary stewardship required for the future of WHO.

Vision

HealthyLong Livesfor All.

A world where everyone can live a long and healthy life, with the care they need, when and where they need it, at a cost they can afford.

Mission

The work, in practice.

To refocus WHO on its core mandates, grounded in science, use its convening power to align partners behind country-led health priorities, and strengthen the Organisation from within by making its financing more predictable, placing staff where their expertise is most needed, and improving internal coordination so WHO can respond more quickly and effectively to countries’ needs.

Pillar 01 — Build

Back to Core Mandates

WHO that delivers on its core responsibilities.

WHO will focus on its core mandates, setting norms and standards grounded in science and helping countries put that evidence into practice through technical support.

Key priorities

✓

Invest in WHO’s scientific expertise. Sustain scientific work where public health needs are greatest, give scientific teams the support they need and ground WHO’s decisions, norms and standards in their scientific evidence.

✓

Help countries put evidence into practice. Provide technical support to help countries adapt WHO guidance to their national contexts and apply it in health policy, public health programmes and clinical practice.

✓

Strengthen governance and responsible use of emerging technology. Help countries navigate rapid advances in artificial intelligence, biotechnology and genomics through clear governance frameworks, responsible data management, and open standards for interoperable health systems, so these technologies improve care and expand access to affordable innovation.

Pillar 02 — Grow

Global Convening around Country Priorities

WHO that brings countries and partners together around country priorities.

Countries’ health sovereignty must be at the heart of global health cooperation. WHO will bring governments and partners together behind country-led priorities, a shared plan and measurable outcomes. It will help align contributions, mobilize domestic financing and ensure external support complements national efforts, with clear accountability for results.

Key priorities

✓

Advance UHC across the life course. Help countries strengthen prevention, primary care and timely referral, supported by trained health workers, so everyone can access quality care without financial hardship, with attention to women’s and children’s health and prevention and management of NCDs, including mental health.

✓

Improve equitable access to affordable health products. Convene countries and partners to advance pooled procurement and market shaping, strengthen regulatory cooperation and expand local manufacturing through technology transfer.

✓

Strengthen health security. Convene national authorities and partners to detect and control outbreaks by strengthening cross-border surveillance, laboratory networks and information sharing, with safeguards for data sovereignty and equitable benefit-sharing, support countries’ climate-resilient health plans, especially in small island states, and sustain essential care through disasters, conflict and displacement with agreed complementary roles for WHO and partners.

Pillar 03 — Scale

Strengthen WHO from Within

WHO that is equipped to deliver and accountable for results.

WHO works best when its people have clear responsibilities, the authority to act and the resources to deliver. WHO will strengthen teamwork and coordination across headquarters, regional offices and country offices so they can serve countries more effectively as One WHO.

Key priorities

✓

Appoint on merit. Select leaders, technical experts, and WHO Representatives based on competence, integrity and ability to deliver, while advancing equal opportunities for women in recruitment and leadership.

✓

Improve accountability for performance and conduct. Set clear performance indicators, review progress regularly, hold managers accountable for results and address misconduct fairly and promptly.

✓

Strengthen WHO’s financial sustainability. Broaden WHO’s funding base and show transparently how resources are used and what they achieve.

“What matters is not only setting direction, but aligning people, mobilising resources and delivering measurable results.”

Accra Reset

A Sovereign Future for Health | Accra Reset

(21/09/26, New York) -- A year ago, the Accra Reset was a call for change. Today in New York, the question is harder: how do we make that change work in practice?

For me, this moment carries a special meaning. Bandung is where I spent my university years and where I met my wife. But the world remembers Bandung for something much larger. In 1955, leaders from Asia and Africa gathered there to affirm political sovereignty—the right of countries to shape their own future. Today, that same spirit is being carried forward: from political sovereignty to health sovereignty.

At the Full Circle convening on the sidelines of UNGA 81, I had the privilege of speaking as Co-Chair of the High-Level Panel on Reform of the Global Health Architecture and Governance, as we launched our report, A Sovereign Future for Health.

At its core, the report is about strengthening country agency over the decisions, financing, and data that shape health systems, while ensuring global cooperation where collective action makes us stronger.

I am grateful to President John Dramani Mahama, my fellow Co-Chairs Peter Piot, Elhadj As Sy, and Priscila Ferraz, and all members of the High-Level Panel for their dedication and collective effort.

The real work begins now: turning strong recommendations into implementation that countries can own and people can feel in their lives.

From Bandung to Accra, it’s time to turn sovereignty from principle to impact, into better health for 8 billion citizens of the world.

(28/09/2026, Jakarta) -- After a full week at UNGA, I returned to Jakarta and went straight back to work.

Tomorrow, I leave for Washington, D.C., to attend the 63rd Directing Council of the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) and the 78th Session of the Regional Committee of WHO for the Americas. Every country’s path is different. But we can learn from one another as we work towards the same goal: healthy, long lives for all.

World Health Summit

Evidence-Based Health Workforce Planning for Indonesia

As a banker for 30 years, it’s easy to say I love numbers. But numbers only matter when they help us make better decisions.

At the World Health Summit in Berlin, during Future Health Scenario Platform - Informing Policy Choice Through Quantitative Modeling, I shared how Indonesia partnered with Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) to strengthen evidence-based health workforce planning.

Indonesia had around 180,000 doctors, but lacked a comprehensive national plan to anticipate future workforce needs. Together with IHME, we developed the National Health Workforce Planning 2023–2032, using epidemiological projections to estimate how many health workers we would need, which specializations to prioritize, and where they should be deployed as our population and disease burden evolve.

This approach helps us move from reacting to shortages to anticipating future needs. We now aim to apply the same principles to other critical areas, including hospitals, medical devices, and medicines.

Our goal is to build a health system that serves all Indonesians, across more than 7,000 inhabited islands, and helps people live longer and healthier lives.

Grateful to share this discussion with Christopher J.L. Murray, Ximena Aguilera, Jane Halton AO PSM FAICD, and Stein Emil Vollset, fellow global health leaders in exploring how data and long-term forecasting can shape better policies and stronger health systems for the future.

BALI · November 2022

Advancing the Pandemic Fund through G20 Leadership

During Indonesia’s 2022 G20 Presidency, Minister Budi led the health track and worked alongside Indonesia’s Minister of Finance, Sri Mulyani Indrawati, to bring together health and finance leaders from across the G20. Together, they built consensus across the G20, whose members included several of WHO’s largest government contributors, on establishing a new fund amid the economic fallout of COVID-19, rising inflation and mounting debt pressures.

That consensus paved the way for the Pandemic Fund, the first multilateral financing mechanism dedicated to providing multiyear grants for pandemic prevention, preparedness and response. The Fund was officially launched in Bali with US$1.4 billion in seed funding committed by 24 donors, including US$50 million from Indonesia. The World Bank recognised the Fund as having been “developed with Indonesia’s and Italy’s leadership during their respective G20 presidencies.”

Impact since launch

By February 2026, the Pandemic Fund had built a portfolio of 67 projects across 128 countries, catalysing more than US$10 billion in additional financing.
